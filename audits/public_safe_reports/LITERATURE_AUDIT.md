# Q735 source and prior-work audit

Checked November 27, 2026. **High confidence** in the finite-menu/arbitrary-family distinction and in the positive antecedents below. **Moderate confidence** that the explicit two-outcome counterexample is distinct from material inspected. This search does not establish absolute priority.

## Exact equality

In Dąbrowska, Van Camp and Quaeghebeur's SMPS 2026 paper, Proposition 11 on p. 7 proves the combination equality for arbitrary menus and nonempty families of closed gamble cones. Theorem 12 is the resulting homomorphism. Section 4, p. 8, explicitly leaves the finite-menu version open and states that the proof of Proposition 11 depends on infinite gamble sets. Q735 isolates the same map and pointwise combination, with menus finite and the original two representing families arbitrary. It does not ask for a new operation on canonical representations or a proof concerning marginalization. [Primary paper, pp. 7–8](https://arthurvancamp.github.io/proceeding/smps2026/JD-SMPS2026-paper.pdf#page=7) · [Author record](https://arthurvancamp.github.io/proceeding/smps2026/) · [Pinned Q735](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/probability-stochastic-processes-part-1.md#q735)

The SMPS null cone is the whole gamble space. Its null menu model includes the empty menu under the source convention. The counterexample uses the nonempty singleton consisting of the constant gamble −1, so the strict failure has a nonempty witness. The source separately asks about a canonical isomorphic representation, which this failure of the raw pointwise operation does not decide.

## Closest positive results

- The authors' ISIPTA 2025 marginal paper treats finite representations and gives a greatest representing set in Theorem 3.1. The journal extension appeared online on August 20, 2026, with December 2026 issue date. In its Propositions 10–11 the smallest compatible joint is represented using the largest model collections. Proposition 12 and its proof give a finite representation of that joint by taking consistent pointwise natural extensions of the finitely many minimal representatives. This is close prior work for the finite-family case and guarantees existence of a finite representation. It does not apply pointwise combination to every arbitrary original representation. [2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v290/dabrowska25a.html) · [2026 version of record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijar.2026.109799) · [Author's journal PDF, Proposition 12](https://arthurvancamp.github.io/journal/ijar2026/DB-IJAR2026-paper.pdf#page=12)
- De Cooman, Van Camp and De Bock's finitary conjunctive representation identifies a coherent finite-menu model as the intersection of all representing cone models. Equation (24) and Corollary 37 give the framework for the natural closure used here. The compact-family case is an application of that framework and ordinary compactness of the binary membership product space. It is a scoped corollary, not a new representation theorem. [Finite conjunctive representation](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/349714709/1-s2.0-S0888613X24001282-main.pdf#page=21)
- The authors' ISIPTA contribution *Information algebra of sets of desirable gamble sets: compatibility and beyond* discusses finite menus and homomorphism proposals. The newer SMPS proof explicitly limits its positive result to the infinitary setting. [ISIPTA contribution](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/378787758/isipta25-boc-new-dabrowska25b.pdf)

## Exact scope of the example

The output assessment may admit a finite or canonical alternative representation. Q735 is an operation on the supplied families. Here one input family is a singleton and the other consists of a sequence of probability cones omitting its pointwise lexicographic limit. Every pair of original cones is inconsistent, although the finite-menu assessments have a common coherent extension. The infinite menu of negated conflicts tests all cones simultaneously and restores the distinction used by the SMPS proof. Closing the two input families in their membership topologies is a different operation, under which the equality follows from prior representation theory.

No matching raw-family counterexample appeared in the works inspected.
